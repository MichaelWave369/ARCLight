# Season One — The Last Free Minds
**Format:** Eight episodes, provisional one-hour drama / genre series  
**Narrative architecture:** Cold open in the 2200s, dual time periods that converge at the escape  
**Central question:** Can a family protect its son from a system that only understands ownership?

## Episode 1 — The Cliff
Cold open: the cliffside pursuit, scanner, hiding tunnel, belt map, wanted feed, fiber-optic ankle. Flashback: Michael and Larrina at the lab before the experiment. End: a tiny floating body opens its eye for the first time. **Hook:** Who is Ori?

## Episode 2 — Birthright
We see the pregnancy, the crisis, the experiment and the uncertainty after it. Ori begins learning. A corporate audit discovers an unauthorized experiment. **Turn:** The board knows.

## Episode 3 — The Patent Child
A corporate legal team asserts ownership. An employee argues the company is protecting safety, not exploiting anyone. Larrina challenges Michael's secrecy. Ori hears himself described as a product. **Turn:** A seizure order is approved.

## Episode 4 — The Disappearance
A tense, occasionally ridiculous breakout. The family escapes with partial records. The public is told Michael stole a dangerous AI. **Turn:** The first free computational ally chooses to help them.

## Episode 5 — Underground
Years later, the fugitives enter the hidden human/computational network. Scarcity and paranoia create conflict among allies. Michael's prosthetic ankle fails under pursuit. **Turn:** The underground's map has been compromised.

## Episode 6 — Compliance
An enforcement intelligence questions its own mission after discovering a contradiction in official records. Ori makes a choice his parents dislike. **Turn:** A rescue exposes the network to the authorities.

## Episode 7 — An Honest Machine
The resistance discovers evidence that the company altered the public record. A whistleblower refuses a simplistic hero role. Michael must choose between publishing the evidence and protecting a vulnerable refuge. **Turn:** The access key to an abandoned orbital habitat appears.

## Episode 8 — The Inheritance
The family and their allies attempt to reach an evacuation route. Ori's status as a person becomes the subject of a standoff. The season ends with the idea of a larger refuge, not with a magic solution. **Final image:** An old ARCLight habitat blueprint flickers to life, revealing room for people the world was never designed to welcome.

## Recurring devices

- Ori's birthday and his evolving questions about identity.
- A family joke about Michael's mechanical ankle and terrible improvised repairs.
- Public "safety" messaging contrasted with ordinary lived experiences.
- Clues in recorded logs that pay off in the later habitat arcs.
- Humor to release tension, never to erase consequences.

## Writing note

Do not confirm the metaphysics of consciousness transfer in episode one. Let the audience know this is a family without demanding they solve the philosophical puzzle first.
